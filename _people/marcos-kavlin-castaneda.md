I am interested in the many forms of ecological restoration, and in particular large-scale restoration across landscapes. Having briefly worked in sustainable development projects, wildfire research, and the forestry industry, I have become greatly interested in the impact the decisions we make as communities can have on the landscape around us.

My research aims to observe the trends in forest regrowth patches through time and determine what socio-economic (as well as biotic and other abiotic) drivers best explain each trend. My study sites include two watersheds, one in Ecuador and the other in Bolivia, allowing a comparison between the different approaches these countries take to restoration. This research is conducted primarily through remote sensing techniques such as Google Earth Engine, with driver analysis conducted using R or Python.

As we seek to scale up restoration efforts in a bid to mitigate climate change, it becomes ever more important to understand restoration across landscapes and its underlying dynamics. Hopefully, this research will help us better understand these dynamics and inform current and future large-scale restoration initiatives.
